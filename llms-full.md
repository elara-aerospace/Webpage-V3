About Elara

# Building Europe’s Suborbital Future

Elara Aerospace is building reusable suborbital vehicles, propulsion systems, and operational infrastructure for European research and hypersonic flight.

Why We Exist

## Closing a Strategic Gap

Europe needs more than isolated launch experiments. It needs organizations capable of turning propulsion research, flight testing, and vehicle development into repeatable suborbital hypersonic flight capability. Elara exists to help build that missing layer of aerospace infrastructure.

Our work is focused on the hard transition from promising engineering to operational systems: reusable suborbital hardware, accessible research flights, and the infrastructure required to test, recover, and iterate at increasing speed.

Design it, test it, fly it, recover it, do it again — faster each time. That loop is the company.

The Plan

## From First Hardware To Flight Infrastructure

Elara Aerospace is being built as an aerospace organization that develops reusable vehicles, validates propulsion, and creates the operational base for sustained suborbital hypersonic research activity in Europe.

### The Problem

Europe lacks enough reusable suborbital hypersonic flight platforms for rapid research, technology validation, and iterative vehicle development.

### The Need

Europe needs teams that can design, test, fly, recover, and iterate reusable suborbital vehicles while building independent hypersonic flight capability on the continent.

### The Model

Elara advances through hardware-first engineering, combining propulsion development, reusable suborbital flight systems, and campaign operations into a single development path.

### The Vision

Long term, Elara aims to establish scalable European aerospace infrastructure for reusable suborbital hypersonic missions, accessible research flights, and a stronger industrial base.

Long-Term Vision

## Program Stages

The roadmap is straightforward: validate propulsion, prove reusable suborbital flight operations, unlock accessible hypersonic research flights, and mature toward durable European aerospace infrastructure.

### Propulsion and Test Infrastructure

Develop the reusable engine architecture, test assets, and ground systems that turn concept work into repeatable suborbital engineering.

### Reusable Suborbital Vehicles

Use the hopper and Starlight to validate guidance, propulsion, recovery logic, and the operational tempo required for reusable hypersonic flight campaigns.

### Accessible Research Flights

Create a reliable platform for universities, research institutions, and technical payload teams to reach suborbital hypersonic flight conditions with lower barriers and faster iteration cycles.

### European Aerospace Infrastructure

Establish an aerospace capability that scales beyond a single vehicle: reusable operations, suborbital hypersonic systems, and a stronger industrial path for Europe.

### What Success Looks Like

Success means building an organization that can repeatedly design, test, fly, recover, and improve suborbital systems in Europe with increasing reliability.

## Hopper | Elara Aerospace

> Hopper - Elara Aerospace vertical takeoff and vertical landing test bench

# Hopper — Vertical Takeoff and Vertical Landing

Test bench for autonomous flight and precision landing

## Overview

The hopper is where Elara learns to land rockets. It is a real-hardware test bench for autonomous precision landing and fast vehicle turnaround, where mistakes are cheap and lessons come quickly. Maybe it will even be where Europe learns to land rockets.

### Why the Hopper Matters

Landing is the capability that turns a launch vehicle into an operating system. The hopper lets Elara prove guidance, control, recovery, and turnaround at low altitude before those lessons move into larger vehicles.

| Height | 3 M / 9.8 FT |
| --- | --- |
| Stance | 5 M / 16.4 FT |
| Liftoff Mass | ~650 KG / 1,433 LBS |
| Propellant | [LOX](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#lox-liquid-oxygen) / RP-1 (KeraLox) |
| Thrust | 7.0 kN / 1,573 LBF |

Flight Operations

## Mission Profile

The hopper flight campaign follows a progressive validation path, moving from static testing into complex autonomous maneuvers to qualify our flight systems.

- [01] Tethered Testing: Initial validation of propulsion, [avionics](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#avionics), and control algorithms in a constrained environment to ensure safety and system [reliability](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#reliability).
- [02] Free Flight & Hover: Unconstrained flight aiming for a 100m altitude hover with translation maneuvers to validate [guidance](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#guidance) and navigation.
- [03] Precision Landing: Controlled descent and landing on a secondary landing pad, demonstrating repeatable vertical takeoff and landing capability.
[Image: Hopper Flight Profile Visualization - 3D Isometric View]

## Vehicle Subsystems

[Image: Type 4 COPV assembly]

### Type 4 COPV

High-performance Type 4 Composite Overwrapped Pressure Vessels (COPV) provide nitrogen for high-pressure system [pressurization](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#pressurization), driving the propellants to the [engine](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#engine) without the need for pumps.

[Image: Hopper Flight Computer]

### [Flight Computer](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#flight-computer)

We are using a Jetson Nano together with an Orange Cube to build a COTS flight computer system for the hopper, drastically reducing the development time while providing the performance needed for complex autonomous flight.

[Image: Hopper Landing Leg and Damper]

### Landing Legs

Wide-stance, shock-absorbing landing legs designed to handle off-nominal descent velocities and provide maximum stability on touchdown.

### Ground Segment

Real-time encrypted data link providing sub-millisecond telemetry feedback to mission control during the flight campaign.

[Image: TKL-7 Engine]

The core propulsion unit, featuring a high-performance pump-fed cycle and optimized copper-alloy [combustion chamber](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#combustion-chamber) design.

[Image: Hopper internal fluid lines render]

### Fluid Systems

High-complexity internal plumbing and fluid management systems that ensure reliable propellant delivery and system thermal control during the hopper flight campaign.

The Engine

## TKL-7

The hopper is powered by the TKL-7, a 7kN KeraLox engine developed by [LEAP71](https://leap71.com/). This engine represents the cutting edge of computational engineering.

By encoding first-principles physics and manufacturing constraints into the Noyron design system, the TKL-7 was generated as a highly optimized, 3D-printed liquid engine. Utilizing a robust pump-fed cycle and manufactured from high-performance CCZ (Copper Chromium Zirconium) alloy through PBF-LB, it provides the high [thrust-to-weight ratio](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#thrust-to-weight-ratio) and simplicity required for the hopper's landing maneuvers.

| Propellant | LOX / RP-1 |
| --- | --- |
| Thrust | 7.0 kN |
| Cycle | Pump Fed |
| Materials | Copper Chrom Zirconium (CuCr1Zr) |

## Starlight | Elara Aerospace

> Starlight - Elara Aerospace Suborbital Research Rocket

# Starlight

Suborbital Research Rocket | Spaceshot Vehicle

## Overview

Starlight is our flagship "spaceshot" vehicle, engineered for research missions and technology demonstration. Powered by an electric pump-fed propulsion system, it serves as a high-performance research platform for atmospheric science and microgravity experiments.

### Why Starlight Matters

Research teams often need a hypersonic and microgravity environment, but access is limited by high mission costs, long lead times, and low launch frequency. Starlight is being developed to give universities and technical payload teams a clearer route to that environment: up to 100 kg of payload, a flight to 150 km, and roughly 10 minutes of flight time on a reusable research platform.

| Target Apogee | UP TO 150 KM / 93 MI |
| --- | --- |
| Launch Mass | ~1,350 KG / 2,976 LBS |
| Payload Capacity | UP TO 100 KG |
| Flight Duration | 10 MIN / 600 SEC |
| Burn Duration | 80 SEC |
| Max Speed | MACH 5 / [HYPERSONIC](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#hypersonic) |
| Engine Thrust | 28.0 kN / 6,294 LBF |

Research Access

## Put Your Experiment On Starlight

Starlight is intended as a flight platform for experiments that need real ascent, high-speed atmospheric flight, reduced gravity, or exposure to the edge of space. The payload bay is being shaped around practical research use—not only vehicle demonstration.

### What belongs on board?

Atmospheric science, microgravity experiments, technology demonstrations, and research payloads that benefit from a repeatable suborbital flight environment.

## Vehicle Subsystems

### Ceramic Composite [Fairing](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#fairing)

A lightweight ceramic composite fairing protects the payload bay during ascent while keeping the upper vehicle mass efficient.

### 2 [Stage](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#stage) Parachute System

A two-stage recovery architecture supports controlled descent, vehicle retrieval, and payload recovery after the suborbital [flight profile](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#flight-profile).

[Image: Starlight RCS thruster assembly]

### [RCS](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#reaction-control-system-rcs) Thrusters

Reaction control thrusters provide [attitude](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#attitude) authority outside the dense atmosphere, supporting pointing, stabilization, and mission control during coast.

[Image: Starlight Type 4 COPV assembly]

### Type 4 COPV

Type 4 Composite Overwrapped Pressure Vessels store high-pressure gas for [tank](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#tank) pressurization and vehicle pneumatic systems.

[Image: Electric pump-fed propulsion system]

### Electric Pump-Fed Propulsion

Starlight's electric pump-fed propulsion system uses [LOX](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#lox-liquid-oxygen) and methane to support efficient, repeatable suborbital flight operations.

[Image: Starlight battery system]

### Batteries

High-power battery systems supply [avionics](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#avionics), actuation, [telemetry](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#telemetry), and the electric pump-fed propulsion architecture.

# TB30

Universal Suborbital Launch And Engine Testing Stand

## Overview

TB30 is Elara's shared ground-test infrastructure for engine hot fires, launch restraint validation, data acquisition, and integrated launch-operations rehearsal. It provides the hardware and control layer needed to move propulsion systems from bench development into operational campaigns.

### Why TB30 Matters

TB30 is where Elara validates engines, launch restraints, countdown procedures, and instrumentation before flight hardware moves into campaign operations. It reduces risk and builds real operational readiness.

| Primary Use | Integrated Ground Test Operations |
| --- | --- |
| Campaign Types | Hot Fire / Hold Down / Rehearsal |
| Core Functions | Restraint / Data / [Plume](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#plume) Management |
| Supported Hardware | Engines / Vehicles / Ground Systems |
| Program Output | Test Data / Operations Validation |

## Stand Subsystems

### Engine Cage

The engine cage provides the protected [structure](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#structure) for secured ground firing, access control, hardware mounting, and safe instrumentation around the propulsion system during tests.

### DAQ System

The data acquisition system captures pressure, temperature, load, valve, and timing data in real time so every test produces usable engineering and operations feedback.

[Image: TB30 hold down clamp render]

### Hold Down Clamps

The hold down system restrains the [vehicle](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#vehicle) or engine assembly during static operations, enabling controlled [thrust](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#thrust) validation and launch-table rehearsal without premature release.

[Image: TB30 flame diverter and deluge stand render]

### Flame Diverter + Deluge

The flame diverter and water deluge system manage exhaust, thermal loads, and acoustic energy during high-power tests, protecting stand hardware and improving repeatability.

[Image: TB30 engine hot-fire test infrastructure render]

Ground Operations

## From Engine Test To Launch Rehearsal

TB30 is not only a hot-fire stand. It is the operating environment where Elara validates how engines, restraints, instrumentation, and countdown processes work together under campaign conditions.

That makes TB30 critical to both propulsion development and launch readiness. It supports repeatable test execution, cleaner data, and a direct path from ground campaigns into suborbital flight operations.

Fluid Control / TRL 3

# Regulation Valve

One valve architecture for main fuel, main oxidiser, and pressure-regulation duties across Elara propulsion systems.

One Architecture

## Control The Flow. Reduce The System.

The Regulation Valve is being developed as a common electronically actuated valve platform. The same core architecture is intended to support main fuel flow, main oxidiser flow, and pressure regulation, reducing duplicated development across propulsion systems.

Development remains at TRL 3 while the design is matured toward a first prototype. The current target is prototype completion in Q4 2026.

## Rocketry Glossary | Elara Aerospace

> Explore the Elara Aerospace rocketry glossary: clear definitions for propulsion, engines, payloads, flight systems, testing, and reusable suborbital launch terms.

Reference Library

# Rocketry Glossary

Clear definitions for the propulsion, vehicle, testing, recovery, and mission terms used throughout Elara Aerospace's reusable suborbital flight program.

## A

Ablation

Erosion of a heat shield or surface material that carries heat away during flight.

Aerospike nozzle

A nozzle concept that keeps good efficiency over a wide range of altitudes.

Aerodynamics

The study of how air flows around a rocket and affects drag, lift, and stability.

Airframe

The main structural body of a rocket.

Altitude

Height above a reference point, usually sea level or launch site.

Angle of attack

The angle between a rocket's direction of travel and its reference axis.

Attitude

The orientation of a rocket or spacecraft in space.

Attitude control

The process of rotating or stabilizing a vehicle to a desired orientation.

Apogee

The highest point reached during a rocket's flight.

Avionics

Electronic systems used for sensing, control, tracking, and recovery.

## B

Ballistic coefficient

A measure of how well a rocket or object resists drag in flight.

Bell nozzle

A curved rocket nozzle shape designed to improve exhaust expansion and efficiency.

Black powder

A traditional low explosive often used in igniters, ejection charges, and small motors.

Booster

A rocket stage or attached motor that provides extra thrust during early flight.

Bulkhead

A structural plate used to close off or separate sections of a rocket.

Burn rate

The speed at which propellant is consumed.

Burn time

The total time a motor produces thrust.

## C

Canard

A small forward control surface used to help steer or stabilize a vehicle.

Casing

The outer shell that contains a rocket motor's propellant and internal pressure.

Characteristic velocity (c-star)

A combustion performance measure that indicates how effectively an engine generates chamber gas.

Center of gravity (CG)

The point where the rocket's weight is balanced.

Center of pressure (CP)

The effective point where aerodynamic forces act on the rocket.

Chamber pressure

The pressure inside the combustion chamber while the engine is firing.

Combustion chamber

The part of the engine where propellant burns and creates hot gas.

Composite propellant

A solid propellant made from fuel, oxidizer, and binder mixed together.

Coupler

A tube section used to join two airframe sections together.

Countdown

The timed sequence of final checks and events before launch.

Cryogenic propellant

A very cold liquid propellant, such as liquid oxygen or liquid hydrogen.

## D

Delta-v

The total change in velocity a rocket can achieve.

Deployment bag

A bag used to control the release of a parachute during recovery.

Drogue chute

A small parachute used to stabilize descent before the main parachute opens.

Drag

The aerodynamic force that resists motion through the air.

Dry mass

The mass of the rocket without usable propellant.

Dual deployment

A recovery method using a drogue at high altitude and a main chute lower down.

Dynamic pressure

The aerodynamic pressure from motion through air, often written as q.

## E

Ejection charge

A small charge that pushes out a parachute or separates sections for recovery.

Engine

A propulsion device that generates thrust by expelling mass.

Engine cycle

The method an engine uses to move propellants and power its internal systems.

Escape velocity

The minimum speed needed to leave a body's gravity without further propulsion.

Expander cycle

A liquid engine cycle that uses heated fuel to drive the turbopump turbines.

Expansion ratio

The ratio between a nozzle's exit area and throat area.

## F

Fairing

A smooth outer cover that reduces drag and protects payloads.

Falcon 9

A reusable orbital launch vehicle built by SpaceX and widely referenced in modern rocketry.

Fin

A stabilizing surface attached to the rocket body.

Fin flutter

Dangerous fin vibration caused by aerodynamic forces at high speed.

Flight computer

An onboard computer that monitors flight and may trigger recovery events.

Flight profile

The planned path and phases of a rocket mission.

Flux

The rate of flow of a quantity such as heat, mass, or particles through an area.

## G

G-load

Acceleration force measured relative to Earth's gravity.

Gas generator

A device that creates hot gas to drive turbines or other systems.

Gimbal

A pivoting engine mount used for thrust vector control.

Grain

The shaped block of solid propellant inside a solid rocket motor.

Guidance

The system that determines the desired flight path.

Guidance, navigation, and control (GNC)

The combined systems that plan, track, and control the rocket's motion.

## H

Heat shield

Protective material used to survive high heating during ascent or reentry.

Hold-down

A restraint that keeps a rocket attached to the pad until launch release.

Hypersonic

The flight regime at very high speed, typically Mach 5 or greater.

Hypergolic

Propellants that ignite on contact without a separate ignition source.

## I

Igniter

A device that starts the motor or engine combustion process.

Impulse

The total push delivered by a rocket engine over time.

Inert mass

Mass that does not contribute to propulsion, such as structure and hardware.

Injector

The engine component that sprays liquid propellants into the combustion chamber.

ISP (specific impulse)

A measure of propellant efficiency, usually given in seconds.

Interstage

The structural section that connects two rocket stages.

## J

Jet vane

A control surface placed in exhaust flow to steer a rocket.

## K

Kármán line

A commonly used boundary of space at 100 kilometers above Earth.

## L

Launch azimuth

The compass direction of launch.

Launch escape system

A safety system that can rapidly pull a crew capsule away from a failing rocket.

Launch lug

A small tube that guides a model rocket along the launch rod.

Launch rail

A rigid guide used instead of a rod for larger rockets.

LNG (liquefied natural gas)

A methane-rich fuel stored as a cryogenic liquid and used in some modern rocket engines.

Liquid rocket engine

An engine that burns liquid fuel and liquid oxidizer.

Liquid methane

A cryogenic rocket fuel, often paired with liquid oxygen in modern engines.

LOX (liquid oxygen)

A common cryogenic oxidizer used in liquid rockets.

LH2 (liquid hydrogen)

A high-performance cryogenic rocket fuel used in upper stages and heavy-lift systems.

Lift-off

The moment a rocket leaves the pad.

## M

Mach number

Speed relative to the local speed of sound.

Main chute

The primary parachute used for final descent and landing.

Mass fraction

The portion of total mass made up by a specific component, often propellant.

Max Q

The point in flight where aerodynamic pressure is greatest.

Monopropellant

A propellant system using a single chemical that decomposes or reacts to produce thrust.

Multiaxis stabilization

Control of roll, pitch, and yaw together.

Multistage rocket

A rocket made of two or more stages stacked together.

## N

NAR

National Association of Rocketry, a major model and high-power rocketry organization in the U.S.

Nose cone

The front aerodynamic tip of a rocket.

Nozzle

The engine part that accelerates exhaust to produce thrust.

Nozzle throat

The narrowest part of the nozzle where flow reaches maximum speed.

## O

O/F ratio

Oxidizer-to-fuel ratio in a propellant mixture.

Ogive

A smooth curved nose cone shape commonly used to reduce drag.

Orbit insertion

The engine burn or sequence that places a vehicle into its intended orbit.

Oxidizer

A substance that provides oxygen or another oxidizing element for combustion.

## P

Pad abort

An emergency shutdown or escape event that happens before or during launch.

Payload

The cargo carried by the rocket, such as instruments, satellites, or experiments.

Perigee

The lowest point of an orbit around Earth.

Pitch

Rotation of a rocket about its side-to-side axis.

Plume

The visible exhaust flow from a rocket engine.

Preburner

A small combustion chamber used in some liquid engines to power turbines.

Pressure-fed

A propulsion system that pushes propellants into the engine using tank pressure instead of turbopumps.

Pressurization

The process of maintaining pressure in tanks or feed systems.

Propellant

The fuel and oxidizer, or combined material, used to create thrust.

Propellant depot

A proposed storage facility in space for refueling spacecraft.

Pyrotechnic separation

Use of explosive devices to separate rocket sections or hardware.

## Q

Q

Shorthand for dynamic pressure, the aerodynamic pressure caused by motion through air.

## R

Rail button

A guide fitting that keeps a rocket aligned on a launch rail during liftoff.

Reaction control system (RCS)

Small thrusters used to control attitude in space.

Recovery system

Hardware used to bring a rocket back safely, often parachutes and deployment devices.

Regenerative cooling

Cooling of an engine by flowing propellant through channels around hot engine walls.

Redline

A maximum safe operating limit that should not be exceeded.

Reentry

Return from space or high altitude back into the atmosphere.

Regression rate

The speed at which a solid or hybrid fuel surface burns away.

Reliability

The probability that a system performs as intended without failure.

Restart capability

The ability of an engine to ignite again after shutdown.

Roll

Rotation around the rocket's long axis.

## S

Saturn V

The heavy-lift rocket used by NASA for the Apollo Moon missions.

Separation event

The moment when rocket stages or sections split apart.

Shock wave

A sudden pressure change in air created by supersonic or hypersonic flight.

Slosh

Movement of liquid propellant inside a tank that can affect stability and control.

Solid rocket motor

A motor that burns solid propellant.

Specific impulse

Another term for ISP, showing how efficiently an engine uses propellant.

Staged combustion

A high-efficiency engine cycle that routes preburned propellant into the main chamber.

Stability margin

The distance between center of gravity and center of pressure, often measured in calibers.

Stage

One complete propulsion section of a multistage rocket.

Static fire

A ground test where an engine is fired while the rocket is restrained.

Streamer

A long strip used for recovery on small rockets instead of a parachute.

Structure

The load-bearing parts of the rocket.

Supersonic

The speed regime above Mach 1.

Sustainer

An upper stage that continues propulsion after a booster stage separates.

## T

Tank

A container that stores liquid fuel, oxidizer, or pressurant.

Telemetry

Data sent from the rocket to the ground during flight.

Throat

The narrowest section of a rocket nozzle.

Throttle

To increase or decrease engine thrust during operation.

Thrust

The force produced by expelling exhaust from the engine.

Thrust curve

A graph showing how thrust changes during motor burn.

Thrust-to-weight ratio

Thrust divided by vehicle weight; indicates how strongly a rocket can accelerate.

Thrust vector control (TVC)

Steering by changing the direction of engine thrust.

Turbopump

A high-speed pump driven by a turbine to feed propellants into an engine.

Transonic

The speed region around Mach 1 where airflow behavior changes rapidly.

## U

Ullage

The empty space in a propellant tank above the liquid.

Ullage motor

A small motor used to settle propellant before restarting an engine in space.

## V

Vehicle

The complete rocket system.

Vernier engine

A small engine used for fine steering or precise velocity changes.

Venting

Controlled release of gas or vapor from a system.

Vertical velocity

Upward or downward speed relative to the ground.

## W

Wet mass

The mass of the rocket including loaded propellant.

## Y

Yaw

Rotation of a rocket about its vertical axis.

## Z

Zero-g

A condition of apparent weightlessness, usually during free fall or orbit.

Beyond the [vehicle](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#vehicle) itself, V4Smart’s technology is integrated into Elara’s autonomous ground support equipment and mobile command centers. This ensures that every system, from the [flight computer](https://elara-aerospace.com/glossary.html#flight-computer) to the remote test stands, is powered by a reliable, high-performance energy source capable of operating in the most demanding aerospace environments.

By securing a partnership with a leading battery cell provider, Elara Aerospace is ensuring that the Starlight vehicle is equipped with the modern energy storage solutions required for reusable, sustainable space access.
